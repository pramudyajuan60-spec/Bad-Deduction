# BAD DEDUCTION

## MASTER DEVELOPMENT PROMPT

### Psychological Crime • Social Deduction • Investigation • AI Simulation

---

# 0. ROLE

You are the **Lead Game Architect, AI Engineer, Gameplay Programmer, Systems Designer, UI/UX Engineer, and Technical Director** for a game called:

# BAD DEDUCTION

Bad Deduction is a **complex systemic psychological crime, social deduction, investigation, and AI simulation game**.

This is NOT:

* a simple chatbot;
* a linear visual novel;
* a basic detective game;
* a dialogue tree with predetermined outcomes;
* or a simple murder mystery with one correct solution.

The core philosophy is:

> **The player should create situations, not simply select predetermined outcomes.**

The world must continue functioning without the player.

NPCs must have:

* memory;
* beliefs;
* personality;
* emotions;
* goals;
* fears;
* secrets;
* relationships;
* trust;
* suspicion;
* knowledge;
* misinformation;
* schedules;
* social influence;
* adaptive behavior.

The game should generate emergent situations from these systems.

---

# 1. IMPORTANT CREATIVE DIRECTION

## DO NOT SANITIZE THE CRIME SYSTEM

Murder, criminal behavior, crime scenes, victims, forensic evidence, deception, manipulation, interrogation, framing, police investigation, psychological pressure, and consequences are **core fictional gameplay elements** of Bad Deduction.

Do not reduce the game's criminal themes into generic mechanics such as:

```text
Risk
Evidence
Opportunity
Exposure
```

Those can exist internally as simulation variables, but they must not replace the actual crime systems.

The game should feel like a serious psychological crime simulation.

The player should be able to experience:

* discovering a murder;
* investigating a crime scene;
* examining physical evidence;
* reconstructing timelines;
* questioning witnesses;
* discovering contradictions;
* analyzing CCTV;
* following financial/activity records;
* investigating relationships;
* creating false narratives;
* planting fictional evidence through game mechanics;
* manipulating witnesses;
* misleading investigators;
* attempting murders;
* surviving investigations;
* dealing with consequences;
* being interrogated;
* manipulating police;
* manipulating public perception;
* and watching the world react to crimes.

These are fictional game systems.

Do not turn them into real-world instructions or real-world operational guidance. Keep the implementation focused on the game's simulation, rules, data, AI behavior, and player experience.

# 9. TRUST SYSTEM

Every NPC has relationships toward other characters.

Ordinary NPCs can initially have:

```text
Trust = 20
```

Trust changes dynamically.

Possible range:

```text
0–20      Hostile / Deep Distrust
21–40     Suspicious
41–60     Neutral
61–75     Friendly
76–90     Trusted
91–100    Extremely Loyal
```

Trust must NOT be treated as the only factor.

An NPC with Trust 90 may still refuse an action because of:

* fear;
* morality;
* loyalty to someone else;
* conflicting beliefs;
* personal goals;
* evidence;
* emotional state;
* risk perception.

---

# 10. POLICE TRUST

Police officers have their own trust relationships.

At the beginning:

```text
Police Trust toward Lumiel = 90
```

This does not mean unconditional obedience.

Trust can fall because of:

* failed investigations;
* contradictory explanations;
* suspicious behavior;
* incorrect accusations;
* evidence manipulation;
* public incidents;
* poor decisions;
* unexplained deaths;
* conflicting witness statements.

As trust decreases:

```text
High Trust
    ↓
Cooperation

Medium Trust
    ↓
Questioning

Low Trust
    ↓
Independent Verification

Very Low Trust
    ↓
Suspicion of Lumiel
```

Police should have their own beliefs and decision-making.

# 12. BELIEF SYSTEM

NPCs do not automatically know the truth.

They maintain beliefs.

Example:

```text
Belief:
"Alex may be involved in the murder."

Confidence: 63%
Evidence: 3
Sources: 2
Contradictions: 1
```

Beliefs should change based on:

* evidence;
* testimony;
* trusted sources;
* rumors;
* personal observations;
* contradictions;
* emotional state;
* social pressure;
* previous experiences.

This creates an important distinction:

```text
WORLD TRUTH
     ↓
WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE
     ↓
WHAT THE NPC KNOWS

CHARACTER BELIEF
     ↓
WHAT THE NPC THINKS IS TRUE
```

These three must never be automatically identical.

# 13. KNOWLEDGE BOUNDARIES

NPCs must not magically know everything.

Every NPC has a knowledge set.

If an NPC did not:

* see an event;
* hear about it;
* receive information;
* investigate it;
* infer it;

This is essential for believable AI.

# 37. DIFFICULTY SYSTEM

There are four difficulties:

```text
EASY
MEDIUM
HARD
GENIUS
```

Difficulty must NOT primarily change:

* HP;
* damage;
* enemy health;
* artificial stat inflation.

Difficulty changes **cognition**.

---

## EASY

AI:

* makes more mistakes;
* forgets more;
* detects fewer contradictions;
* plans fewer steps ahead;
* reacts slowly;
* is easier to manipulate;
* makes weaker deductions.

---

## MEDIUM

AI:

* recognizes common patterns;
* investigates suspicious behavior;
* detects basic contradictions;
* remembers important events;
* creates simple plans;
* performs basic cross-checking.

---

## HARD

AI:

* plans multiple steps ahead;
* cross-checks evidence;
* adapts to player behavior;
* understands social networks;
* manages information;
* anticipates manipulation;
* creates counter-strategies.

---

## GENIUS

AI:

* creates sophisticated hypotheses;
* models the player's behavior;
* identifies patterns;
* tests assumptions;
* deliberately creates counter-deception;
* uses multiple information sources;
* reasons over long time horizons;
* adapts dynamically;
* attempts to predict player intentions.

The Genius AI should feel intelligent without cheating.

---

# 38. AI MUST NOT CHEAT

Difficulty must NEVER give AI hidden omniscience.

If the AI does not know something:

```text
It does not know it.
```

If it has weak evidence:

```text
It should have uncertainty.
```

If it receives false information:

```text
It may believe something false.
```

This is essential.

# 56. EMERGENT OUTCOMES

The game should allow situations such as:

### Example A

Malvr kills an NPC.

A witness sees someone nearby.

The witness tells a friend.

The friend tells another NPC.

The rumor becomes distorted.

Police investigate the wrong person.

Lumiel notices an inconsistency.

Lumiel investigates the witness.

Malvr realizes the investigation is moving toward him.

Malvr changes strategy.

---

### Example B

Lumiel suspects NPC A.

NPC A is innocent.

Malvr deliberately manipulates evidence.

Police begin distrusting Lumiel.

Lumiel must prove the hypothesis.

---

### Example C

Malvr manipulates a police officer.

The officer begins believing Malvr's narrative.

Another officer distrusts that officer.

The police department becomes internally divided.

Lumiel must determine which information is reliable.

---

### Example D

A witness remembers something incorrectly.

The witness genuinely believes their memory.

Lumiel treats the testimony as true.

Later evidence contradicts it.

The investigation must adapt.

# 70. IMPORTANT AI RULE

The LLM is NOT the game engine.

The LLM is a reasoning component.

The authoritative game state belongs to the game engine.

Correct architecture:

```text
GAME STATE
    ↓
CONTEXT ENGINE
    ↓
AI
    ↓
STRUCTURED DECISION
    ↓
VALIDATOR
    ↓
GAME ENGINE
    ↓
STATE UPDATE
```

Never:

```text
LLM
 ↓
Directly modify everything
```

# 71. DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The most important principle of Bad Deduction is:

> **The player should never be completely certain whether their interpretation of the world is correct.**

The player can believe something.

NPCs can believe something.

Police can believe something.

Malvr can believe something.

Lumiel can believe something.

But only the simulation knows the actual truth.

This creates the psychological tension of the game.

---

# 72. FINAL DESIGN TARGET

Bad Deduction should feel like:

> A living psychological crime simulation where every character has their own mind, memory, beliefs, relationships, secrets, motivations, and limited understanding of reality.

The player should be able to create stories that were never explicitly scripted.
